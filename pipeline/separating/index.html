2. Separating populations in the shape space¶

Stage 2 answers two questions without labels: which fibre populations are present in a window, and where each of them is, voxel by voxel. Its readers are fitted on the block itself.

The teacher¶

The excised structure tensor at the output ρ is the teacher: at its confident sites (the top 30% by orientation gap in the tissue, drawn evenly over orientation bins so that no direction dominates) its direction is taken as that voxel's orientation. No labels, no annotation. The least oriented 30% of the tissue give a background pattern.

Two readers of the shape space¶

The signature dictionary is the mean standardised feature pattern of every orientation (160 directions on a hemisphere, kernel-smoothed), plus the background pattern. The signature of any direction is interpolated from it.

The orientation readout is a small network from a voxel's features to a distribution over the same 160 directions, trained with the teacher's direction as a soft target. Summed over a window, the voxels' distributions are the window's orientation density, whose peaks are the candidate populations.

Both are fitted by shapetensor.pipeline.calibrate on one block (real data) or on a set of single-population blocks (phantoms at several orientations), and both are applied unchanged to any block of the same data.

Which populations: candidates confirmed by their layers¶

The readers do not respond equally to every direction (the network's filters are not isotropic), so both are gain-normalised per direction against the teacher on the calibration block: the density by the ratio of what the readout reports to the teacher's own orientation histogram, the activations by what a sample reaches on its own direction's signature. Every direction then counts alike.

The density's peaks still over-count: a single population with dispersed fibres has shoulders that a peak finder reads as further populations. The pipeline therefore gathers up to six candidates — the peaks of the block's density and of its 64³ sub-windows' (a block may hold regions of different orientation) — and lets stage 3 judge them. Each candidate is first unmixed alone into its activation layer (below), and the layer's own population tensor — the image's gradients weighted by that activation — gives the direction its fibres actually have; the shoulders of one population all point at that population, a real second population points elsewhere, and the distinct axes (25° apart) are kept. The kept axes are then unmixed together, twice, and the distinct axes kept again: under a joint unmixing the shoulders lose their shared voxels to the population they belong to. A joint unmixing never has more than four signatures (the rank of the orientation code is five), and a population must own at least 5% of the tissue above the activation null.

Where each population is: the activation layers¶

Every voxel's feature vector is decomposed non-negatively onto the signatures of the populations found plus the background:

\[x(v) \approx \sum_r a_r(v)\, D(\theta_r) + b(v)\, D_\text{bg}, \qquad a_r \ge 0\]

The coefficients \(a_r(v)\) are the activation layers: one full-volume image per population of where that population's shape is, not a mask of the image. They are what stage 3 weights the image gradients with and what stage 4 uses as weights. The null \(\tau\) — the activation a wrong population's signature reaches on single-population voxels, calibrated on the block — is exported with every field; it is never applied to the vectors.

The crossing row of the validation figure shows the layers on a phantom; the real data figures show them on HiP-CT and BigBrain.

What this stage cannot do¶

With the second-order orientation code of the features (stage 1), a voxel whose receptive field holds two populations looks like a weaker single population at their bisector, and three populations are linearly dependent. Separation therefore works where the populations interdigitate at the fascicle scale, and it degrades as the crossing angle closes and as the fibres within a population disperse: see validation.
