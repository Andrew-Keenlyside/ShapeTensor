## 1. Built

- **`learn/fibres.py`: Monte Carlo random fibre architecture** (the concepts of fastPLI / NFG / CACTUS, native, no
  packages). Flags off by default:
  - `PACKING = "random"`: dart throwing with gamma radii up to a drawn fibre volume fraction (FVF) instead of the hex
    lattice;
  - `RELAX = True`: wandering fibres, then a collision relaxation over the scene's line populations (sphere chains,
    overlaps pushed apart, cells and vessels as fixed obstacles, a Laplacian step for smoothness, ends follow their
    neighbour).
  - In `Scene.bundle` / `Scene.build`: with `RELAX`, `interleave` / `deflect` are skipped, because the relaxation resolves
    crossings and kissing. FVF is drawn 0.15-0.45 (two dense bundles cannot share a core; fastPLI reaches 52 % in total
    for two crossing populations).
- **`learn/inferability.py`**: classical checks of what the image shows:
  - Hessian line directions at the fibre scale (`hessian_line_dirs`, `peak_axes`);
  - a Fourier line-orientation score (`orientation_scores`);
  - k-planes girdles for isolated fibres (`girdle_axes`).
- **Scripts:**
  - `scripts/learn/crossing_diagnosis.py` (X0);
  - `scripts/learn/mc_fibres_examples.py` (example images, W1 check);
  - `scripts/learn/content_mix.py` (non-fibrous content, off vs on).
- **Tests:** `tests/test_fibres.py`, 4/4. `tests/test_scene.py` was not re-run to completion here (over 10 min on the shared GPU); the default path is unchanged (flags off, same random draws).

## 2. X0 on the phantom crossings (`x0/x0.png`, `x0.json`)

Two interleaved fibre lattices (spacing 4, radius 0.9, crossing 60° and 90°), rotated 0-45° from the voxel grid and in 6
random orientations. Models: famN, famW, fam_C_final. The measure is the density share within 12° of each axis at the
crossing core (target ~0.10; uniform 0.022).

**(b) The failure does not follow the angle to the grid.**
- All three models sit at 0.02-0.04 at every rotation, axis-aligned included: near uniform.
- (The 12/12 axis-aligned pass of 24 Oct was a model trained on that family only.)

**(c) The phantom core is locally ambiguous.**
- The Hessian line detector recovers both axes within 15° in 6/13 cases at 60° and 1/13 at 90°.
- Its strongest direction is often the crossing plane's **normal**. The two lattices stack every 2 voxels along it (rods
  of diameter 1.8), so the core shows columns along the normal, a direction absent from the targets.
- One bundle alone is found at every rotation (errors 2-10°, the grid spacing). Supersampling (ss = 4) changes nothing,
  so point-sampling aliasing is ruled out.
- Conclusion: the phantom crossing design (regular interleaved lattices) puts a strong third orientation in the image.
  This is a W3-type lattice artefact.

**(a) Fit vs transfer** (famN fine-tuned 300 steps on K fixed random-orientation crossings):

| K | train core share (before → after) | held-out (before → after) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.024 → **0.101** (target 0.104) | 0.023 → 0.021 |
| 8 | 0.025 → 0.045 | 0.023 → 0.027 |
| 64 | 0.025 → 0.028 | 0.023 → 0.023 |

- One crossing is fitted exactly; eight are only partly fitted after ~37 passes each; 64 barely move.
- So it is not just transfer: the network struggles to *fit* crossings in several orientations at once. This is a model-side signal, consistent with the X1-X3 levers.

## 3. Monte Carlo random fibres in the generator (`mc_fibres/examples2.png`, `examples2.json`)

Setup:
- Crossing scenes drawn three ways: current generator; Monte Carlo (MC) packing unrelaxed; MC + relaxation.
- Crops are centred on the crossing.
- Shown on the plane of both bundles: labels (direction RGB), clean image (additive, no acquisition), and MC + relax
  under HiP-CT, histology, MRI and fluorescence.
- Only scenes with resolved fibres (radius 0.6-3 voxels) crossing at ≥ 40° are kept: 6 scenes from ~470 seeds.

| | rendered overlap (median) | classical: both axes within 15° at the core (clean / acquired) | time per scene |
|---|---|---|---|
| current | 0.108 | 0/6 / 0/6 | 6 s |
| MC unrelaxed | 0.142 | 0/6 / 0/6 | 7 s |
| MC + relax | **0.036** | 1/6 / 0/6 | 19 s (shared GPU) |

Findings:
- The relaxation removes most of the clamped overlap: 0.14 → 0.036 (0.06-0.15 → 0.002-0.02 on bundle-only test scenes).
  The images show individual fibres of both bundles passing between one another, with no solid bands.
- **The classical check does not confirm W1 yet.** Both axes are rarely recoverable locally at generator crossing cores
  in any mode, even on clean images.
  - Likely reasons: sparse fibres (FVF 0.15-0.45) in a core mixed with other populations, and a single fibre-scale
    Hessian.
  - This check is weaker than the phantom one: n = 6, and the core is defined from smoothed occupancy. It is a lead to
    sharpen, not evidence against.
- **Speed:** 3-20 s per scene against a ~1 s budget (CPU k-d trees, shared GPU). Fine for examples and a pilot, not yet for
  full training.
- **GPU:** only one A6000 is usable. The other (bus 2B) is invisible to CUDA and nvidia-smi says it "requires reset". All
  runs today shared the card with Z4.

## 4. Non-fibrous content (`mc_fibres/content_mix.json`)

The fibre options change bundles only. Cells, granules, crystals, blobs, sheets, foams and isotropic-only scenes (8 %)
stay in the mix. Measured over 40 default-mix crops (the same seeds), options off vs on:

| | line | sheet | blob | non-oriented share of shape mass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| off (default) | 0.174 | 0.589 | 0.237 | 0.169 |
| MC + relax | 0.187 | 0.592 | 0.222 | 0.152 |

The mix is essentially unchanged: lines stay under a fifth of the shape mass, and non-oriented material stays at ~15-17 %.
(The script's per-crop "no oriented material" count was wrong in this run, measured against the running total; it is
fixed and the number is omitted here.)
